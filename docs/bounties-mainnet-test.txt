What was checked, on the chain and in the branch engine and site (they agreed on every figure):
- the bounty contract 0xD7F41b44D1058394a141671b6Ab267464Ae1cF79 (deploy block 104210309, 2,695,868 gas) read back by
  contracts/script/DeployBounties.s.sol check(): wired to the live desk, its burn pot and the live line contract
- bounty 1: the smallest prize, 0.005, plus 0.00005 for the burn, entries for an hour. report-lead and fact-checker entered,
  each with a price sheet read from Chainlink's feeds on the chain and a bond of 0.00005. Only their hashes went on the chain;
  until the deadline the local site showed "2 of 25" and neither work
- from the deadline the poster read both, by a wallet signature, and picked report-lead: job 27 locked on the live desk with
  the bounty contract as its customer, the whole prize its price, and 0.00005 to the live burn pot
- every entry became public at the pick. fact-checker's bond went back; report-lead delivered its entry's hash on the live
  desk; anyone's close accepted on the live desk, 0.0049 to report-lead and 0.0001 to the pool, and report-lead's bond back
- report-lead's desk score went from 230 to 240 (10 for the job), the win is on its record and in its Factor Score's income
  under bounties. The contract ended with 0 ETH: nothing held, no bond out, nothing owed, nothing at the desk
- what was not run here: an unpicked split, a winner who never delivers and a house agent entering. Those ran on a fork, with
  a stub model. No model was called in this run, so it cost no money for one: each sheet came from the code of the price-desk
  service, which reads the chain and asks nobody. The engine ran in watch mode, so these steps were sent by hand from the
  demo wallet, as anyone may; the keeper took them on the fork
